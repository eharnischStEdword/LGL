Fund Giving Dashboard v2

Design proposal · St. Edward Church, Nashville
August 10, 2026
Prepared for Eric Harnisch

The recommendation: one scrolling page that opens by answering the three questions staff walk in with, with a new weekly panel built for the Monday meeting. No tabs, no rewrite. Every calculation the dashboard makes today survives unchanged; what changes is that the answers move to the top, the caveats become visual marks instead of paragraphs, and the 42-fund wall becomes one ranked list. Three design directions were developed and scored against each other; this is the winning hybrid. The mockups below are working HTML, drawn in the exact visual language v2 would ship.

1What v1 gets wrong

A full pass over the current interface (grounded in the code, not vibes) found these. The math behind every number checked out; the problems are all presentation and placement.

HighThe comparison views never actually compare. YoY and FY Compare render one look-alike card per fund-year ("Offertory (2025)", "Offertory (2026)"). You find the pair, subtract in your head, and judge the gap yourself. The one number the view exists to produce, the difference, is never shown, even though it is already computed.

HighThe page is upside down for its audience. Above the fold: a logo, a metadata line, ten buttons in three styles, the top of a chart. The only plain-English summary (the Financial Snapshot) is dead last, below the fund chips. The up/down trend verdicts sit under the chart like footnotes.

HighThere is no weekly view at all. Everything is bucketed by month, so the freshest thing visible on a Monday morning is a partial month, and the Monday question ("how were the last few weeks?") is unanswerable. The gift-level dates needed for weekly totals are already loaded in memory.

HighLoading All Funds shows you one fund. The All Funds report auto-selects Offertory only, and the parish-wide total hides behind an unlabeled chip at the bottom of the page, styled like just another fund. Nothing says "you are seeing 1 of 42 funds."

HighTwo competing 40-row fund controls. The Fund Totals table and the 42-chip selector both toggle chart membership. The chips give a ten-gift mission collection the same weight as Offertory, and unselected chips are light gray on lighter gray.

MediumCaveats are paragraphs, not marks. The honest-comparison logic (completed months only, partial month excluded) is explained in 30-word gray sentences that get skipped, a full screen away from the asterisk they explain.

MediumOne flat row of mixed controls. Six time pills (two of which are secretly whole different views), a Chart/Table toggle, Line/Bar, and Log all sit together in four visual styles. The default range is "Last 12 Mo" though parish staff think in fiscal years. A "Last 24 Mo" range exists in code with no button.

2The shape of v2

Answer first, evidence second, controls on demand. The entry screen goes away (data loads on arrival); the page reads top to bottom in meeting order.

2 · Evidence

Monthly trend with its regression trendline, Compare Years, or Table.
3 · Detail
Fund ledger
One ranked list replaces the chip wall and the totals table.
4 · Utility
Bulletin & export
Copy for Bulletin lives inside the briefing, where the numbers are.

3The Monday briefing

This is the new top of the page, and the reason v2 exists. The week runs Monday through Sunday and is labeled by its ending Sunday, because the Sunday collection is what the week builds toward. On a Monday morning the just-ended week is always still being counted (plate and checks usually post Monday or Tuesday), so it can never be the headline: the last complete week carries the headline and all comparisons, and the counting week is visibly second-class until the Wednesday after its Sunday.

What the marks mean: gold stripes always mean "in progress," never a value judgment. The counting week gets no red arrow and no year-over-year percent, ever; a half-counted week compared against a full week reads as a collapse. The dashed blue line is the 4-week average of complete weeks, which is the honest weekly trendline (single weeks are noisy: five-Sunday months, snow, travel). Prior-year week comparisons use the week exactly 364 days back so day-of-week lines up; weeks containing Christmas or Easter show a "holy-day week" flag instead of a misleading percent. The banner deletes itself every Wednesday.

Merging the two views also kills the hardcoded 2025/2026 in the YoY code, one of the two deferred bugs on the books; the merged view computes its years.

5The fund ledger: All Funds without the wall

The chip grid and the Fund Totals table become one ranked, searchable list, collapsed behind a header that is itself information. Ranked totals with share bars do the "which funds matter" job that 42 equal-weight chips cannot.

All Funds fixed at the source: when the All Funds report loads, the chart defaults to the All Funds (Total) line with Offertory overlaid, and a permanent scope line under the chart title says exactly that ("Showing: All Funds total + Offertory · 2 of 42 funds"). Checking ledger rows overlays more funds; past 5 the summary tiles collapse to top-5 plus "N more," past 8 the legend hides and the chart grows tall, exactly as v1 already behaves.

6One visual language

v1 uses color and type by accident: four caption grays, cents everywhere, fund colors and red/green verdicts six pixels apart, serif and sans traded at random. v2 gives every channel one job and never reuses it.

ChannelIts one meaning
Gold "In progress, be careful": hatched bars, counting badges, scope pills, the Monday freshness dot. Never a fund color, never a verdict.
Green / red Direction of a delta, nothing else. "New this FY" is gold (nothing rose), and a fund with no giving either period says so in gray instead of rendering nothing.
St. Edward blue The All Funds (Total) identity, everywhere it appears: pinned ledger row, total line, 4-week average overlay.
Series colors Fund identity on chart marks only, from a four-step palette derived from the brand and validated for colorblind separation and contrast (green #00843D, blue #2A6FB0, gold #B8860B, red #B23A2F). Beyond four charted funds the fifth-plus render gray with direct labels, replacing today's 25-color cycle. Text never wears a series color.
Type & dollars Georgia for headline money and section titles; Trebuchet for everything else. One scale: 11px captions, 13px labels, 15px body, 24/34px money. Whole dollars on tiles, lists, and chart labels; cents only in tables, tooltips, and the bulletin payload. Two grays, not four.
Caveats Marks, not paragraphs: scope pills on the numbers they scope, hollow dot + dashed final segment + "Aug*" tick for the month in progress on every view (today only YoY gets this), "* month in progress" written in the chart legend where the asterisk lives, and ⓘ tooltips holding the methodology sentences.

7Everything that stays

The brief was keep all the logic and math, and the design does. Verified against the code:

Monthly bucketing and local-time date parsing
Regression trendline, dashed, stops at last complete month
Per-fund prior-period trend math (now shown with dollars)
Completed-month clipping in all year comparisons
Historical backfill rules (live data always wins)
Fund ranking and grand total (feeds the new ledger)
Collision-aware chart labels, negative formatting
Log scale with $0 gaps (moves into chart options)
Months-by-years pivot table, FY and CY
Financial Snapshot math + Copy for Bulletin, byte-for-byte
Fund search, 6-fund and 8-fund crowding behaviors
Entra sign-in, 5-minute server cache, brand palette

8Build order

Five steps, each independently shippable on the existing single-file app. No big-bang restructure; components get extracted only as each section is touched.

1
Launch gate: fix the API date filter
The server's recent-gifts top-up filters by gift update date instead of gift received date (the known deferred bug). For monthly buckets that error is rounding noise; on a weekly axis it is a whole bar, on exactly the morning the meeting looks at it. Switch to the received-date filter and verify against the live LGL key first. Also confirm both LGL scheduled reports export at least 53 weeks of rows so "vs same week last year" can populate.
2
Visual language pass + Compare Years cards
Pure restyling of already-computed values: the delta grammar, scope pills, one type scale, merged compare view (which also removes the hardcoded 2025/2026).
3
Answer band + masthead auto-load
Kill the entry screen; load All Funds on arrival with the Offertory hybrid fetched in parallel and a plain fallback banner if the big report fails. Financial Snapshot content absorbs into the briefing.
4
Recent Weeks panel
Client-side weekly bucketing from data already in memory (needs step 1). Week logic gets the same adversarial verification the monthly math got in the June audit: week boundaries, FY rollover, the 4-week average, the 364-day lookback.
5
Fund ledger merge + control cleanup
Chips + totals table become the ranked ledger; controls become two labeled groups (Period / View) defaulting to This FY; Line/Bar/Log move to a corner cluster; dead "Last 24 Mo" code path gets a button or gets deleted.

Three calls to make (recommendations included)

  • Weekly panel scope: one fund at a time, defaulting to Offertory, with a dropdown for any fund or the parish total. Weekly resolution across 42 funds would rebuild the wall this redesign removes. (Recommended: yes.)
  • Auto-load All Funds as the only entry: slightly slower first paint, and the big zip export has broken once before; the fallback banner covers it. The alternative keeps the two-button screen. (Recommended: auto-load with fallback.)
  • Fund category groupings (Parish / School / Diocesan): skip at launch. The groups do not exist in LGL data, so they would be a hand-kept list that goes stale every time a fund is added. The ranked totals already communicate importance. (Recommended: skip.)

Sources: heuristic review of src/Dashboard.jsx and server.js; three independently developed design concepts ("The Monday Brief," "Four Desks," "The Monday Page") scored on clarity, Monday fit, All Funds fix, and feasibility; winning hybrid synthesized from the top scorer plus the best elements of the other two.
